# v2 competitive gaps and argument spine

Read the closest substantive works before claiming novelty. Compare 5–10 candidates when available on RQ, population, educational stage, geography, design/sample, operational constructs, theory, findings, limitations, overlap and residual difference. With fewer studies log a shortfall rather than padding. Record passage evidence, not only author names.

State a falsifiable tentative contribution; identify the strongest already-existing rival; argue against novelty; defend only a specific substantive remainder. Return GAP_SUPPORTED, GAP_NARROW_BUT_DEFENSIBLE, GAP_NOT_SUPPORTED or INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE, bounded to searched sources/contexts. Distinguish author-claimed gaps from researcher inference and empirical checking. Geography/population/buzzword/mixed-methods substitutions alone do not warrant novelty. An already-answered question without a defensible conceptual remainder yields NOVELTY_NOT_SUPPORTED; narrow the RQ or make no novelty claim.

Build 3–7 main comparative claims and an economical editorial spine: thesis, logically ordered subclaims, transition warrants, opponent/response, modest conceptual contribution, novelty boundary and introduction → review → framework → methods → implications alignment. Each claim must resolve to at least two independent deeply read works, their actual positions, checked evidence IDs, comparison/warrant W#, objection O#, qualified own inference, scope, relative strength, theory/RQ/section links and a paragraph plan. An author-name collage fails G3.

Graph relationships: supports, contradicts, qualifies, extends, different_context, method_incompatible, same_construct_different_measure (plus dependency links). Each content relationship needs passage evidence. Retain declared negative/opposing findings explicitly; do not turn them into consensus or drop the inconvenient work at quota selection. Distinguish reported/tested/observed/potential claims. Framework and method-fit audits must pass before substantive argument acceptance.
